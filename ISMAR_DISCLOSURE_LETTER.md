## Use conditions

The current IEEE ISMAR author guidance requires authors to provide anonymized related work and an anonymous explanation when a submission is closely related to work under review, including another submission at the same ISMAR. Recheck the 2027 CFP and author instructions before submission:

<https://www.ieeeismar.net/2026/guidelines/author-guidelines>

**Verified 2026-09-06.** The 2025 wording of this requirement was: authors whose submissions build on
unpublished work under review elsewhere "must provide these anonymized works including an anonymized letter
outlining the relation to the ISMAR submission," and this applies to concurrent submissions at ISMAR itself.
ISMAR 2025 also ran a **single track** — reviewers assign the outcome (TVCG paper / conference paper / poster /
reject) and "the authors shall not indicate the intention in the submission." No cap on submissions per author
group was found. Re-verify all of this against the 2027 instructions; track structure and wording change
between editions.

**Note the poster outcome.** Because a single track can return "poster" rather than "reject," a companion
paper that reads as thinner than its sibling can be down-ranked to a poster — which then counts as prior
publication for a later full-paper attempt. Submit two papers only when both are full-strength.

The preferred study design uses independent cohorts and independent experiments. That makes the scientific separation strong, but the papers remain closely related in apparatus and topic and should still be disclosed if concurrently under review.

Select exactly one cohort statement below. Never claim independent cohorts if any participant appears in both studies. Never claim prior ethics approval, preregistration, or planned separation unless the dated records support it.

## Purpose

In accordance with IEEE ISMAR's requirement to disclose closely related work under concurrent review, we report a companion submission that uses the same research platform and addresses a related but distinct research question. This letter describes the shared material and the non-overlapping contributions.

## The submissions

### Submission 1 — [final Paper 1 title]

Submission 1 evaluates warning design during an immersive reach/dodge task. Its confirmatory experiment crosses warning policy (proximity-triggered versus TTC/prediction-triggered) with haptic spatial mapping (generic torso versus at-risk limb), with no-warning and trigger-matched visual benchmarks. Its primary outcome is whether the designated target limb enters the designated virtual hazard during a scripted opportunity.

### Submission 2 — [final Paper 2 title]

Submission 2 estimates the physically realized vibrotactile warning lead time required to prevent an ongoing upper-limb virtual-hazard crossing. A separate tactile stop-signal task estimates action-cancellation latency, and a controlled lead-time × movement-tempo experiment tests whether that latency and current movement speed explain the warning threshold. Its primary analysis is a trial-level hierarchical psychometric model.

## Non-overlap

The submissions have different:

- research questions;
- experimental tasks and confirmatory trials;
- manipulated variables;
- primary outcomes;
- statistical models;
- hypothesis families; and
- result tables and figures.

Submission 1 does not estimate a psychometric lead-time threshold or tSSRT. Submission 2 does not analyze or report Submission 1's six-condition task comparison. Neither submission requires the companion submission to obtain a positive result.
