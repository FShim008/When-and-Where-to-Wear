ABSTRACT  (197 words)
================================================================================

Head-mounted displays hide the room, so systems warn users when a tracked body
part nears a real object. When that warning should fire has one published test,
and its result is uncomfortable: a cue triggered by forecast time-to-contact
produced MORE collisions than one triggered at a fixed distance. That study
varied two things at once. Its warning also grew and faded with the user's
speed, so slowing down turned the warning down, and participants crept forward
managing the signal instead of stopping. Trigger and display cannot be told
apart in that design. We separate them. [N] participants each completed six
blocks of [18] scripted collision opportunities in room-scale VR, among real
obstacles rendered invisible. Crossing trigger policy (fires on proximity
versus on forecast time-to-contact) with cue mapping (the limb at risk versus
an undifferentiated torso site) isolates timing while the cue itself is held
identical; a sixth condition holds the forecast trigger and varies only whether
the cue is a single pulse or a continuously modulated one. Forecast triggering
[changed] violation odds by [OR, CI]; continuous modulation by [OR, CI];
limb-localized cueing by [OR, CI]. [Closing sentence — see below.]

THE CLOSING SENTENCE — WRITE IT FROM H4', NOT FROM H1
================================================================================

The paper's contribution is a RESOLUTION, so the last line must say what was
resolved. Three outcomes, three sentences, all publishable:

  H4' shows continuous modulation is WORSE:
    "The earlier result is attributable to the display rather than the trigger:
     a warning the user can turn down by slowing is obeyed less, whenever it
     fires."
    -> the strongest outcome. It explains a published null.

  H4' null, H1 shows forecast triggering HELPS:
    "Forecast triggering helps when the cue cannot be modulated, so the earlier
     result does not generalise to discrete warnings."
    -> still resolves the discrepancy, by scope rather than by mechanism.

  H4' null, H1 null:
    "Neither trigger policy nor cue form accounted for the difference, so the
     earlier result rests on the task or the effector rather than the warning."
    -> honest, and narrows the field for whoever looks next.

NEVER write a closing sentence that presumes which of these happened.

NOTES ON THE WRITING
================================================================================

WHAT IT CLAIMS, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
  It does NOT claim to be the first comparison of trigger policies — that claim
  is false and was caught late (Valkov & Linsen, IEEE VR 2019). It claims to
  SEPARATE two explanations that prior work confounded. That is a stronger
  position: both outcomes are informative.

  It does NOT claim an optimal warning time. D = 0.30 m and T = 1.0 s are
  representative operating points of two policy classes, and the paper says so
  (docs/PAPER1_PARAMETER_JUSTIFICATION.md §3).

WHY THE PRIOR RESULT LEADS
  Opening with someone else's inconvenient finding is unusual and it is the
  right call here. It gives the paper stakes a reviewer can see in two
  sentences, and it pre-empts the "this was already done" objection by making
  that work the reason the paper exists.

  Do not name the authors in the abstract — say "one published test". Naming
  belongs in Related Work, where the treatment can be fair. Also, ISMAR is
  double-blind and this is not our own prior work, so naming is permitted; the
  reason to avoid it is rhetorical, not procedural.

THE SENTENCE THAT CARRIES THE METHOD
  "isolates timing while the cue itself is held identical" — this is the whole
  contribution in eleven words. If a reader takes one clause away, it should be
  this one.

WHAT IS DELIBERATELY ABSENT
  - No mention of Paper 2. The papers stand alone.
  - No presence/workload/sickness numbers. They are secondary and equivalence-
    framed; putting them here would imply they were tested for difference.
  - No H3. It is exploratory at 57% power and does not belong in an abstract.
  - No alert-burden number, despite it mattering: it is a cost measure without
    a hypothesis, and the abstract has no room to frame it honestly.

LENGTH
  197 words. ISMAR abstracts run 150-250. Paper 2's is 188. If it must shrink,
  cut the second and third sentences of the prior-result setup to one.
