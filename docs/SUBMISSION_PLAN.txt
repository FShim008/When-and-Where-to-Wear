================================================================================
WHY NOT BOTH AT ISMAR
================================================================================

Submitting two is ALLOWED. ISMAR sets no per-author limit and the two studies
are genuinely distinct — different task, different cohort, different question.
Dual-submission rules turn on overlap, and there is none. Policy is not the
problem.

Three other things are.

1. PAPER 1 IS NOT READY SCIENTIFICALLY
   - H4 is invalid; the Visual benchmark needs rebuilding
   - H2 is confounded; the Tactosy question is unresolved
   - T = 1.0 s rests on simulation, not on any human measurement
   - the title "What Makes a Collision Warning Work?" promises a timing answer the design
     does not give

   These need thinking time. Thinking time is exactly what disappears when
   you are running five sessions a week.

2. THE PARTICIPANT MATH LEAVES NO SLACK
   66 participants at ~90 min each. If IRB clears 1 Nov, that is 12.5
   effective weeks after the holidays, so 5.3 sessions per week for both.
   Paper 2 alone is 2.4/week — comfortable. Both is a grind with zero room
   for a no-show, an equipment failure, or a bad pilot finding.

3. THE DEPENDENCY RESOLVES BETTER WITH A SPLIT
   Paper 1's weakest sentence is how T was chosen. Split the venues and
   Paper 1 can cite Paper 2's ACCEPTED result:
       "T was set to exceed the LT80 of X ms reported in [Paper 2]."
   That is the strongest available version, and it is only available if
   Paper 2 lands first.

================================================================================
WHAT THIS COSTS, STATED PLAINLY
================================================================================

Paper 1 is submitted about six months later than if both went to ISMAR 2027,
and appears roughly a year later.

That is the whole cost. Against it:
  - Paper 1 goes out with H2 and H4 fixed instead of known-broken
  - T is grounded in measurement instead of simulation
  - Paper 2 gets February undivided
  - no 5-sessions-a-week grind
  - no overlap or anonymity questions at either venue

A rejected paper costs a full cycle. A deliberately delayed one costs six
months and comes back stronger.


================================================================================
GATES — THE ONLY REASONS TO REVISIT
================================================================================

  1 NOV 2026    IRB not yet approved?
                -> Paper 2 main study slips. If approval lands after 1 Jan,
                   ISMAR 2027 is at risk and Paper 2 moves to IEEE VR 2028
                   with Paper 1 following at ISMAR 2028.

  1 DEC 2026    Fewer than 8 Paper 2 participants collected?
                -> pace is wrong. Diagnose recruitment before December ends.

  1 FEB 2027    Paper 2 data not complete?
                -> stop collecting, analyse what exists, decide whether the
                   interval is usable. §16.2 already allows "too wide to
                   specify" as a publishable outcome.

Nothing else reopens the venue decision. In particular, do NOT add Paper 1
back into the March deadline in January because things are going well —
that is how both end up rushed.


================================================================================
ALTERNATIVES CONSIDERED
================================================================================

  Both at ISMAR 2027
      Allowed, and tempting. Rejected: Paper 1 would ship with H2 and H4
      unresolved and T ungrounded, to save six months.

  Paper 1 at ISMAR 2027, Paper 2 at IEEE VR 2028
      Backwards. Paper 1 is the one that is not ready, and it is the one
      that benefits from the other going first.

  Both at IEEE VR 2028
      Wastes the ISMAR 2027 cycle entirely for no gain.

  Paper 1 to ISMAR 2028 instead of IEEE VR 2028
      Fine as a fallback if the IEEE VR deadline is missed. Same logic holds.
